Work Experience

Three jobs so far: one in the public sector, two freelance for local businesses. Every one of them was the same problem wearing different clothes — people were doing real work on paper, in notebooks, or in WhatsApp groups, and my job was to turn it into a system that does not lose anything.

Times below are approximate; the exact months are in the résumé on my desktop.

Web Developer

2022 – present

Auditoría General de la Provincia de Salta — professional, full time

What they do

The provincial audit office receives the annual financial accounts of every municipality in Salta, stores them, checks them, and turns them into the statistics the province publishes. My work is the software half of that: collecting the data, storing it, auditing it and showing it to the public.

What I built

  • An internal system to submit, store and search municipal financial data for the current and previous years, and to generate the province-wide statistics that come out of it.
  • A full audits module: work programs, the audits themselves, and generating the final reports the office signs off on.
  • A public-facing web application so anyone can look at the collected data in a form that is actually readable, instead of asking the office for a copy of a PDF.
  • A municipal communication application that replaced physical (paper/mail) submissions with digital ones, with national file signature validation so a submission can be traced and trusted.
  • The plumbing in between: the MySQL schema, the Spring Boot API, the deployment on AWS, and the boring parts of the app that keep the auditors from filing tickets.

My role

  • Frontend-heavy full stack: React and Tailwind on top of a Spring Boot API and a MySQL database I also designed.
  • Translator between the auditors and the code. Most of my best requirements arrived as paperwork, and my job was to work out what the screen should be.
  • Shipped in small pieces, in production, for real users, while the old process kept running alongside.
  • Handled the parts nobody asks for: input validation, audit trails, permissions, and not losing anybody's data.

Freelance Web Developer

2025 – 2026

Lavandería del 13 — Sistema del 13 — freelance

What they do

A laundry two blocks from my house. Orders on paper, payments in a notebook, and no way for a customer to find out whether their clothes were ready without walking over and asking.

What I built

  • Client, order and payment management, so the shop stopped keeping its whole business in a notebook.
  • Automatic notifications to clients when an order is ready, which is the single feature the owner mentions first.
  • A dashboard for the owner with statistics and reports: volume per day and week, revenue, and which services actually get used.
  • Historical lookups, because "did we already wash that?" was a daily question.

My role

  • Everything, end to end: database, API, interface, deployment.
  • Talked to the owner in person, in the shop, and then iterated on the real screens rather than the ones in my head.

Freelance Web Developer

2026

Punto Mobile — Sistema de Gestión — freelance

What they do

A mobile phone shop: stock coming in from providers, phones going out, half the stock sitting in repair trays, and a lot of money changing hands without anything written down.

What I built

  • Stock and inventory management, including what came from which provider.
  • Sales and payments: what was sold, how it was paid for, and who still owes what.
  • Repairs as a real workflow: intake, diagnosis, parts, state of the device, and the promise date promised to the customer.
  • Providers: a ledger of what the shop owes each one, which was the thing that made the difference for them.
  • An owner dashboard with statistics, plus reports of the emails sent and received, delivered through Mailgun.
  • Self-hosted on a small DigitalOcean box, because a single shop does not need a bigger bill.

My role

  • Requirements, data model, API, interface, deployment and the support calls that follow.
  • Kept the data entry fast: most of the interface exists so the person at the counter can type once and move on.

How I Work

  • I start from the process as it exists today, on paper if necessary. The software that matches the paperwork gets adopted; the software that replaces it gets argued about.
  • Small pieces, in production, early. A working screen beats a perfect branch.
  • I own the whole path, database included, because the bugs that hurt live exactly at the seams between the parts.
  • I care about the boring stuff: validation, audit trails, backups, and what the screen does when the request is slow.
  • I write things down. If it only exists in my head, it does not exist.
